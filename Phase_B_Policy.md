# Phase B: Policy Document

**Policy for Synthetic Media Use in University Athletics Communications**

---

## Preamble

This policy governs the use of synthetic media by the university athletics communications office. It applies to all full time staff, part time staff, student interns, contractors, and vendors producing content on behalf of the office. It takes effect on the date of adoption and supersedes any prior informal practice. It is written to be read on a Monday morning and acted on the same day.

The office produces content that represents the university, its athletic programs, its student athletes, and its coaches. That content carries the university's name and reputation. Synthetic media tools make it possible to produce convincing video and audio of people who did not participate in the recording. This policy defines when that capability may be used, when it is refused, and how the office accounts for its use.

---

## Section 1: Definitions

**Synthetic media.** Any video, audio, or image in which a person's voice, face, or identifiable characteristics are generated, altered, or reproduced by artificial intelligence or machine learning tools rather than captured in a live recording. This includes voice cloning, face swapping, lip syncing, avatar generation, and fully AI generated human figures.

**Identifiable characteristics.** Any feature that a reasonable viewer could use to identify a specific person, including face, voice, gait, distinctive clothing, jersey number combined with position, or any combination of features that narrows identity to one person.

**Institutional identity.** The university's name, logos, colors, trademarks, athletic marks, team names, and any visual or verbal element that a reasonable viewer would associate with the university.

**Disclosure.** A clear, plain language statement that content is synthetic, placed in a location and format that a viewer will encounter before or during viewing.

**Provenance.** Metadata, cryptographic signatures, or content credentials attached to a file that identify its origin and production method.

**Review board.** The three person panel defined in Section 6.

---

## Section 2: Permitted Uses

The office may use synthetic media only for the following purposes, and only when the conditions in Sections 4, 5, 6, and 7 are met.

**2.1 Accessibility and translation.** Generating synthetic voice narration of existing approved written content for accessibility purposes, such as text to speech for visually impaired audiences, or translation of existing approved statements into other languages. The original speaker must consent, the script must match the approved source exactly, and the output must be disclosed.

**2.2 Anonymized instructional content.** Generating synthetic human figures to demonstrate techniques, drills, or plays in instructional or educational content where no real athlete, coach, or staff member is depicted and no real person's likeness is used.

**2.3 Placeholder and draft content.** Generating synthetic media for internal review purposes only, clearly marked as draft, never distributed outside the office, and deleted once the final production is complete.

**2.4 Historical or archival reconstruction.** Generating synthetic voice or imagery to accompany archival content where the original recording is damaged or incomplete, subject to review board approval, consent from the person depicted or their estate, and prominent disclosure.

All other uses are prohibited unless the review board grants a specific written exception under Section 6.4.

---

## Section 3: Prohibited Uses

The office refuses the following uses outright, regardless of intent, consent, or disclosure.

**3.1 No synthetic depiction of any real person saying something they did not say.** This prohibition is absolute. No exception process exists. A real coach, athlete, staff member, or administrator may not be represented as speaking words they did not speak, even if the words are accurate, even if the person consents, even if the content is disclosed as synthetic. The reason is that the office's credibility depends on the audience's ability to trust that a person shown speaking actually spoke.

**3.2 No synthetic depiction of any real person in any context that could be mistaken for a live or recorded event.** This includes press conferences, interviews, game footage, commitment announcements, and social media statements.

**3.3 No synthetic depiction of a student athlete.** Student athletes are in a distinct category of vulnerability. They are minors or young adults, they are subject to NCAA rules, and their name, image, and likeness rights are governed by contract and law. The office will not generate synthetic media depicting any student athlete under any circumstances.

**3.4 No synthetic depiction of any person without their documented written consent**, except as permitted under Sections 2.2 and 2.3.

**3.5 No synthetic media that depicts the university's institutional identity without review board approval.** This includes any use of the university's name, logos, colors, or athletic marks in a synthetic production.

**3.6 No synthetic media intended to influence recruiting, whether of student athletes, coaches, or staff.** Synthetic commitment videos, synthetic coaching pitches, and synthetic campus tours are prohibited.

**3.7 No synthetic media produced during an active crisis or breaking news situation.** During a crisis, the office produces only verified, sourced content. Synthetic tools are not used until the review board has met and authorized a specific use.

**3.8 No synthetic media of any person who has not reached the age of eighteen**, regardless of consent.

---

## Section 4: Consent Workflow

When a real person's voice, likeness, or identifiable characteristics are involved, and the use is not prohibited under Section 3, the following workflow applies.

**4.1 Who obtains consent.** The office's designated consent coordinator, currently the Assistant Director for Content, obtains consent. No other staff member may obtain consent.

**4.2 Form of consent.** Consent must be written, signed, dated, and specific to the proposed use. The consent form must state the purpose, the medium, the distribution channels, the duration, and the disclosure language that will accompany the content. Verbal consent is not sufficient and is not accepted.

**4.3 Documentation.** Signed consent forms are stored in the office's secure consent log, accessible to the review board. Each entry records the person's name, the date, the specific use authorized, the expiration date, and any conditions.

**4.4 Duration.** Consent expires twelve months from the date of signature unless the form specifies a shorter period. Consent for a specific project expires when the project is complete and the content is removed from distribution.

**4.5 Revocation.** Any person may revoke consent at any time by written notice to the consent coordinator. Upon revocation, the office removes the content from all channels it controls within five business days and documents the removal. The office cannot recall content that has been downloaded or re shared by third parties, and this limitation is stated in the consent form.

**4.6 Consent from estates.** For deceased persons, consent must be obtained from the estate's legal representative. The same documentation and duration rules apply.

**4.7 No consent, no production.** If consent cannot be documented, the project does not proceed.

---

## Section 5: Disclosure Standards

**5.1 In artifact disclosure.** All synthetic media produced under this policy carries a disclosure that is present in the artifact itself, not only in surrounding context. Acceptable forms include a spoken acknowledgment at the start of audio, a persistent on screen label for video, and a visible watermark for images.

**5.2 Placement.** The disclosure appears within the first three seconds of video or audio, and remains visible or audible throughout. A disclosure placed only at the end is not sufficient.

**5.3 Wording.** The disclosure states plainly that the content is synthetic. Acceptable wording includes "This content was generated using artificial intelligence" or "This video contains AI generated elements." Euphemisms, technical jargon, and vague language such as "enhanced" or "digitally assisted" are not acceptable.

**5.4 Downstream sharing.** Staff who distribute synthetic media to external partners, media outlets, or platforms must include a written notice that the content is synthetic and must request that the notice travel with the file. The office cannot guarantee downstream compliance, and this limitation is acknowledged in Section 8.

**5.5 Metadata and provenance.** All synthetic media produced under this policy is exported with content credentials enabled when the production tool supports them, and with descriptive metadata embedded in the file name and file properties identifying the content as synthetic.

---

## Section 6: Review and Approval

**6.1 Review board composition.** The review board consists of the Director of Athletics Communications, the office's legal counsel or their designee, and one rotating member of the communications staff appointed for a six month term.

**6.2 Standard of review.** The review board approves a proposed synthetic media use only if all of the following are true. The use falls within Section 2. It does not fall within Section 3. Consent is documented under Section 4. The disclosure plan meets Section 5.

**6.3 Timing.** Proposals must be submitted at least five business days before production is scheduled to begin. Urgent requests may be considered by the Director alone, but only for uses under Sections 2.1 and 2.2, and the full board reviews the decision at its next meeting.

**6.4 Exceptions.** The review board may grant a written exception to Section 2 only when the proposed use advances a compelling institutional interest, no real person's likeness is involved, and the disclosure plan is robust. Exceptions are documented, time limited, and reported in the annual audit under Section 9. The board may not grant exceptions to Section 3.

**6.5 Records.** All proposals, decisions, and rationales are recorded in the office's synthetic media log and retained for three years.

---

## Section 7: Incident Response

**7.1 What counts as an incident.** An incident includes any of the following. Synthetic media produced under this policy reaching an audience without its required disclosure. Content being attributed to a person who did not authorize it. A consenting person revoking consent after distribution. A tool producing content that misrepresents the office's intent. Any external party misusing content the office produced.

**7.2 Immediate response.** Any staff member who becomes aware of an incident reports it to the Director within twenty four hours. The Director convenes the review board within two business days.

**7.3 Corrective action.** The review board determines corrective action, which may include public correction, removal of content from all channels the office controls, direct notification to affected persons, notification to platforms hosting the content, and suspension of the tool or workflow that produced the incident.

**7.4 Documentation.** Every incident is documented in the synthetic media log with a written account of what happened, what was done, and what the office will change to prevent recurrence. The documentation is reviewed at the next quarterly staff meeting.

**7.5 External communication.** Public statements about incidents are issued only by the Director or their designee, in coordination with the university's central communications office.

---

## Section 8: Refusal Conditions

The office declines to produce synthetic media, even for uses that Section 2 would otherwise permit, under any of the following conditions.

**8.1 When the office cannot verify the truth of the content.** If the script or claims have not been fact checked against a documented source, the project does not proceed.

**8.2 When the office cannot guarantee the disclosure will travel with the artifact.** If the distribution channel strips metadata, removes labels, or re encodes content in ways that eliminate the disclosure, the office does not produce for that channel.

**8.3 When the subject of the content is a student athlete, a minor, or a person who has not consented in writing.**

**8.4 When the use would require the office to represent the university's institutional identity in a synthetic production without review board approval.**

**8.5 When the request comes during an active crisis, breaking news event, or period of institutional stress.**

**8.6 When the person requesting the content is not able to articulate a specific purpose that falls within Section 2.**

**8.7 When any member of the review board records a written objection on ethical grounds.** A single objection is sufficient to pause the project until the objection is resolved in writing.

---

## Section 9: Audit and Enforcement

**9.1 Quarterly audit.** The review board audits the synthetic media log every quarter. The audit verifies that all production followed Sections 4, 5, and 6, that all disclosures were present, and that no prohibited uses occurred.

**9.2 Annual report.** The Director submits an annual report to the university's central communications office summarizing synthetic media use, incidents, exceptions, and any recommended changes to this policy.

**9.3 Enforcement.** Violations of this policy are addressed through the office's standard personnel process. Intentional violations of Section 3 are grounds for termination and may be referred to the university's legal office.

**9.4 Sunset clause.** This policy is reviewed in full every eighteen months. If the review does not occur, production of synthetic media is suspended until the review is complete.

---

## Section 10: Adoption and Communication

**10.1 Effective date.** This policy takes effect on the date of adoption by the Director of Athletics Communications.

**10.2 Training.** All staff complete a one hour training on this policy within thirty days of adoption and annually thereafter.

**10.3 Questions.** Questions about the policy are directed to the review board. Questions about specific projects are directed to the consent coordinator.
